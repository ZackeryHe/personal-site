# Concept: 卷 — "The Scroll"

## The core idea

A Chinese **handscroll** (手卷) is never viewed all at once. You unroll it an arm's length
at a time, right to left, and the painting reveals itself as a journey: mountains, a path,
a pavilion, a river crossing. Then at the very end come the **colophons**, where later
viewers wrote their notes and stamped their seals.

That matches all three of your instincts:

| Your idea | How the scroll metaphor uses it |
|---|---|
| Watercolor / ink, things appear as you get closer | Distance = mist. Far things are pale washes; near things are dark, confident brushstrokes. Ink "bleeds" into existence at the edge of view. |
| 3D, walk around, contained, single path | You walk *the path painted in the scroll*. One path, scroll-wheel/arrow keys move you forward and back, and the mouse lets you look around. Nothing to get lost in. |
| Rooms / hallway / museum / outside | Mostly outside, with **one interior**: a pavilion that works as the gallery. It's outdoors and museum at once. |

## You are the brush

The world starts as blank paper. A single drop of ink falls in front of you and the
landscape blooms out from it: the ground first, then the trees, then the mountains, then
the distant ranges. As you walk, ink keeps falling from where you stand, so you literally
paint the scroll by walking it. The places you've been stay painted. Clicking flicks ink
somewhere else, so visitors can explore ahead.

The blank space ahead isn't empty: it's **drawn but not yet painted**. Ink paintings are
often made in this order: first the outline (勾, 白描 line drawing), then the washes (染).
So beyond the ink, within ~75 m, the landscape is a light sepia line drawing (unpainted
shapes left as paper, 留白), and the ink floods into those outlines as you arrive. In the
opening, the sketch races out from the first drop and the washes follow behind it.

## Stand still: the colour stage

A painting gets its outline first (勾), then its ink (染), and its **colour last (设色)**.
The walk does the first two by itself. The third is a reward for pausing: stand still and
crane your head to the side, and colour blooms where you look: mineral blue-green on the
mountains (the 青绿 "blue-green landscape" style), green on the leaves, a spring wash on the
ground, teal water, flowers springing up toward you, butterflies arriving. It comes in
faint and fills in while you keep looking, and the pointer's dot of ink takes in the colour.
The places you lingered stay coloured, so by the end of a visit the scroll records where you
stopped to look.

## The walk

1. **The greeting**: while the world is built, a great brush hangs overhead in a dim, empty room,
   a thread of incense smoke curling up beside it and a drop of ink gathering at its tip; your
   name waits below. Nothing of the world shows out here: it's all inside the scroll. Click
   anywhere: the drop falls into a handscroll lying unrolled below, and the world paints itself
   out over the scroll from where it lands, until it's all you see.
2. **Contacts, at the lotus pond**: three small carved rocks on its near shore, **GitHub ↗**,
   **LinkedIn ↗** and **Email ↗** (click them).
3. **Experience**: an avenue of stone tablets (碑林), one per role, each carved with it (click one
   and you read it), oldest first: UC Irvine, AWS, Forge Rewards, Databricks, Databricks again. Between whitewashed garden
   walls (粉墙) with dark tile coping, lattice windows (漏窗) and a moon gate (月洞门), bamboo
   peeking over. The waterfall is already visible at the end of it.
4. **Projects**: stone lanterns lining the path through the bamboo; the three that are lit, warm,
   are your projects, their names floating over them (click one and you read it).
5. **The waterfall**: you come out of the bamboo facing it. It comes down between two
   mountains: twin peaks after the old painters (Dong Yuan, Huang Gongwang, Fan Kuan), folded
   with rocky ribs, smaller peaks breaking their ridgelines, flanks flaring out at the foot, long
   hemp-fibre strokes (披麻皴) running down their slopes, a belt of cloud drifting across them;
   the falls pour from the face of the summit set back between them into a pool, mist billowing
   up where they land; cranes wade in the shallows, a fisherman drifts on the pool, deer graze
   by its stream, which runs under a stone arch bridge and winds away across the meadows.
6. **Resume**: at the bridge, floating over a crane wading in the pool's shallows on its left.
   Open it and the resume unrolls on a hanging scroll (立轴): a cord, a rod, silk round the
   paper, a roller with jade knobs, like the scroll the drop falls into at the start. Across the
   bridge, the pavilion on the pool's shore, the pagoda on its knoll behind, the falls beside it.
7. **Source code (源码)**: across the meadows, a well under its own little roof, a windlass and
   bucket over it: 源, the source (a spring's, and this site's: Source code ↗). Then to the foot
   of a rock escarpment, and up a sky ladder (天梯) of stone steps cut straight up its face,
   steeper and steeper, to the rim.
8. **The end: nothing built but a stone bench, on a narrow crest high on the rim, right at the
   edge.** The lookout is a promontory: to either side the ridge falls away into the sea of
   cloud, its ink thinning into the paper as it goes (留白). The crest is narrow and the face
   below it steep, so that sitting there you look over its brow straight down into the valley
   and see none of the climb. The land stops
   at a cliff with a lone pine on the brink; no flowers grow and no petals fall around the
   bench, so it's just you and the view. Below, a canyon that is a forest of stone: sheer towers rising out of
   a sea of cloud (云海) that you look down onto, their feet lost in billowing cloud, their
   ledges and crowns dotted with scrub (点苔, the dabs painters use for distant vegetation) and
   here and there a pine, a natural stone arch (天生桥) between two of them, a waterfall leaping
   into the cloud. They step back in layers, each
   paler than the last, to the far ranges and a huge red sun (红日) setting in the saddle between
   them, its warm glow washed across the sky and the haze, cranes gliding across its face. The
   sun is the walk's one big accent of cinnabar, and you catch it from far back: beside the
   waterfall, behind the pavilion as you cross the bridge.
9. **The painting (留念)**: sit on the bench and you turn round, and there's the whole walk laid
   out below you as one painting: the bamboo, the bridge and the pavilion, the waterfall between
   its peaks and the pagoda beside them, the ranges behind. Look left and the stream from the
   bridge sweeps across the meadows in long bends (a pale wash between dark banks), slips behind
   a craggy rock standing up out of the meadow with a pine on its crown, the near dark mass that
   anchors that side, and winds away, narrowing, to the foot of a far massif where a thread of
   falls comes down the cleft between its two peaks: one rock near, one fall far, and the mist
   between. Further round, out over the valley beyond the rim's foot, a long low bank floats in
   the mist in the middle distance, a few pines along its crest, one standing taller, and a
   smaller, paler one beyond it: a few trees on a bank, the rest left empty. In it all, the ink
   you left as you
   walked and the colour where you stopped to look, so no two are the same. Any view from the
   bench is yours to keep: look around (right round to the canyon and the sun), rest your eyes on
   one and it's framed, and one click puts it on your clipboard, sealed, with a line of colophon.

Along the way, birds come down to peck ahead of you and scatter as you approach, and rabbits
sit up in the grass, then bolt. Petals drift down around you: plum blossom early on, gold and
orange leaves toward the end, where more and more of the trees are turning (colour them in and
they come out gold, orange and red instead of green). As you near one of your points, your
head is gently nudged toward it.

The entrance is left open: no gate, no carved name stone in the way, just the garden and, far
off to the right, the red sun you'll walk toward. (A moon gate is still an option, see backlog.)

## Visual rules (so it stays coherent)

- **Palette:** paper (warm off-white), ink (blue-black at varying density), *one* accent:
  cinnabar seal red. Maybe a pale indigo/ochre wash used sparingly.
- **No hard 3D lighting.** Shading is "ink density," not shadows. No textures that look
  photographic.
- **Mist pools at the bottom** of things (classic 留白, "leaving white"). Empty paper is
  part of the composition — don't fill the scene. The mountains rise out of it, their feet
  dissolving into the paper; the canyon's sea of cloud thins away as it comes in to the rim.
- **Mountains in layers, not mounds:** each is ridges standing one behind another, every ridge
  inked darkest along its crest in a blue-black, an indigo wash up high and warm ochre down its
  slopes (浅绛), thinning toward its foot, so the crest of the ridge in front stands out against
  the pale slopes of the one behind it.
- **Type:** a calligraphic face for occasional characters, a quiet serif for English.

## Alternatives considered

- **Museum hallway only:** cleaner to build, but loses the "appearing out of the mist" magic,
  which is the strongest part of your idea.
- **Open world you can roam:** more impressive, much more work, and visitors get lost
  instead of reading your stuff. A single path keeps it a website.
- **Four seasons along the path** (spring → winter): a nice twist that could layer onto
  the scroll later, e.g. sections change season.

## Idea backlog

- **Moon gate entrance** (圆洞门): a short white wall with a round opening that frames
  the first view. More "garden", less "wild".

- **The pavilion's hanging scrolls:** the tablets carry your roles and the lit lanterns your
  projects now; the six scrolls in the pavilion are still blank. A line of poetry on each?
- **Guestbook seals:** at the cliff edge, visitors stamp a small seal of their own. That's the
  colophon tradition; it needs a tiny backend later.
- **Sound:** wind, the waterfall, a few guqin notes when a section writes itself in.
- **Time of day along the walk:** paper warms toward dusk as you approach the setting sun at the end.
- **Calligraphy on the tablets:** a line of poetry brushed in as you arrive.
- **"Skip the walk" view:** plain, fast page with the same content (recruiters, slow phones).

## Open questions

- What actually goes on the site? (resume, projects, writing, photos, links?) This decides
  how many "stations" the path needs.
- Who's the main audience: recruiters, friends, clients? Recruiters need a fast
  "skip the walk" button / plain-HTML fallback.
- Do you want real Chinese text in it (your name, section titles), or keep it visual-only?
- Mobile: swipe to walk is supported in the prototype, but is mobile a priority?
